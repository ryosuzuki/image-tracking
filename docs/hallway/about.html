The wall becomes the interface.

Scan a QR, allow your phone’s rear camera, and point at a square marker under a paper. A white outline marks a paper you can tap. Its original pages, author film, and selected research demonstrations stay in the marker’s local frame. You can browse everything without a camera, too.

What stays anchored?

This release tracks visible OpenCV ArUco DICT_4X4_50 markers. The paper is A4 portrait, centered 18mm above an 80mm marker (black square, not the white border). Keep the marker in view. Overlays disappear within half a second when it is lost; media pauses. This is not persistent room mapping or an anchor remembered after walking away. Depth and phone intrinsics are estimated, not a physical calibration.

iOS Safari and Android Chrome can use the regular camera API over HTTPS. WebXR is not required. A browser’s camera permission and a real hallway lighting/device test are still necessary. No camera frames are uploaded or saved.

Reused sources

  • Spatial’s existing Unity SharedFrameNativeMedia / NativeGaussian and Three.js prototype-demo: paper identity, original PDF/video sources, scene export, original Ryo Gaussian/rig/motion/narration.
  • Ryo’s image-tracking repository, markers/web-webxr: js-aruco2 detector, OpenCV 4×4 dictionary, planar homography and projection code.
  • MemoryDiorama: original Unity Sakura and Balloons mesh hierarchies, materials, spline paths. Three.js adaptation, without streamed terrain or photo-to-3D generation.
  • Ryo’s daily-demo Gaussian: deterministic 40k-splat mobile derivative of the original 160k model, with original covariance/color and rig. It is not a replacement MetaPerson or body-scan avatar. Mobile sorting and rendering are reduced; this is not full desktop Gaussian quality.
  • Original author films and PDFs are linked from their public source hosts. A YouTube-only film opens on YouTube. No movie-study clip or private room capture is redistributed.

Video2MR replays the original paired tennis video and animated FBX, with time-linked motion, pause, speed and restart. CinemaWorld replays the original Unity envelope particle samples without redistributing the movie-study clip. The application does not claim every Unity demo is ported. Supported research scenes have a Demo action; other papers retain original pages, films when available, and the original Ryo avatar. The site does not fabricate a demo where no source port is present.
